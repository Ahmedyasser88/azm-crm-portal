## Dependency notes

Stories 01–04 implement KAN-1 ("Customer Management Module"), split to mirror the corresponding backend delivery increments in the `azm-crm-backend` sibling repo (which has its own KAN-1 squad plans, split identically: core CRUD, interactions, notes, attachments). Story 01 builds the customer list/detail/create/edit/delete pages and is a hard prerequisite for 02–04, since each of those edits the `/customers/[id]` page and extends the shared `lib/api/customer.api.ts` / `app/(pages)/customers/actions.ts` files Story 01 creates. Stories 02, 03, and 04 are independent of each other and can be implemented in any order — each adds one self-contained section (interaction history, notes, attachments) to the same detail page and depends only on its own corresponding backend story being deployed.

Stories 05–07 implement KAN-2 ("Ticket Management System"), again mirroring the backend's own three-story split (core CRUD, assignment, status/escalation). Story 05 builds the ticket list/detail/create/edit pages, adds the "التذاكر" sidebar entry, and is a hard prerequisite for 06–07, since both edit `/tickets/[id]/page.tsx`, `/tickets/page.tsx`, `TicketFilters.tsx`, and `lib/types/ticket.ts`. Story 05 also depends on Story 01 (KAN-1) for `customerEndpoints` — ticket creation requires searching existing customers, and the ticket detail page resolves the owning customer's name. Stories 06 and 07 are independent of each other and can land in either order; each adds one section (assignment, status/escalation) to the same ticket detail page and depends only on its own corresponding backend story being deployed.

Stories 08–09 implement KAN-3 ("Communication Channels Integration"). Unlike KAN-1/KAN-2, the frontend split does **not** mirror the backend's five-story split (core/web-form, email, WhatsApp, SMS, live chat) one-to-one — the backend's Stories 09–11 (email, WhatsApp, SMS) each add only a new outbound `IChannelMessageSender` and an inbound webhook against Story 08's shared `Conversation`/`Message` model, requiring **zero** additional frontend work beyond what Story 08 already builds (a channel-agnostic conversations/messages UI). Story 08 therefore covers the frontend surface for four of KAN-3's five acceptance criteria (email, WhatsApp, SMS, web forms) in one story, reusing the ticket module's `CustomerPicker`/`searchCustomersAction` as-is. Story 09 covers only the fifth criterion, live chat, which is architecturally distinct enough (a real-time SignalR hub connection, the app's first-ever client-side/browser network call) to warrant its own story; it depends on Story 08's conversation detail page and types, and on the backend's Story 12 specifically (not 09–11).

Stories 10–13 implement KAN-4 ("Agent Dashboard"), mirroring the backend's own four-story split (dashboard core, agent tasks, quick reply templates, ticket collaboration) one-to-one, since each backend story introduces its own independent controller/entity with no cross-story coupling on the backend side either. Story 10 replaces the placeholder `/dashboard` page with a real one showing the caller's assigned tickets (with an embedded customer summary per ticket) and headline counts — covering two of KAN-4's five acceptance criteria ("View all assigned tickets in one place", "Access customer information from the dashboard") and establishing the `app/(pages)/dashboard/page.tsx` shell that Stories 11 and 12 each append one more panel to, the same way KAN-1 Stories 02–04 each added one section to the customer detail page. Story 11 adds a tasks/reminders panel to that same dashboard page ("Manage tasks and reminders"), Story 12 adds a quick-reply-templates panel to it plus a template picker wired into the existing conversation message composer from Story 08 ("Use quick reply templates"), and Story 13 — independent of the other three, since it touches only the ticket detail page — adds an internal comments section to `/tickets/[id]` ("Collaborate with team members"). Stories 11, 12, and 13 can be implemented in any order relative to each other; only Story 10 is a hard prerequisite for 11 and 12 (both edit the file it creates).

Stories 14–17 implement KAN-5 ("SLA & Automation Engine"), mirroring the backend's own four-story split (SLA policies, auto-assignment rules, escalation rules, breach alerts) one-to-one — each backend story is a self-contained CRUD resource (or, for Story 17, a read-only list) with no cross-story coupling on the backend side. Story 14 adds a new top-level `/automation` page and sidebar entry, establishing the `app/(pages)/automation/page.tsx` shell (stacked `.card` panels fed by one `Promise.all`, the same accumulating pattern Story 10 established for `/dashboard`) that Stories 15–17 each append one more panel to; it also surfaces the SLA due-date fields the backend's Story 17 appends to `Ticket`/`TicketListItem` (an SLA card on `/tickets/[id]` and a client-computed "متأخرة"/overdue badge on both `/tickets` and `/tickets/[id]`), and shares one `AutomationActionResult` type (defined in its `slaPolicyActions.ts`) that Stories 15–16's own actions files import rather than each redefining. Story 15 adds an auto-assignment-rules panel ("Auto-assign tickets based on rules"), reusing `AssignTicketControl.tsx`'s raw-agent-id-input convention since no list-agents endpoint exists anywhere in this codebase. Story 16 adds an escalation-rules panel ("Configure escalation rules for overdue tickets") — its effect (automatic ticket escalation) requires no new ticket-detail UI at all, since an automatically-escalated ticket renders identically to a manually-escalated one via KAN-2 Story 07's existing `EscalateTicketControl`. Story 17 adds a read-only breach-notifications panel ("Trigger alerts and notifications for SLA breaches") — the actual alerting (email) is entirely backend-driven, so this story's only frontend surface is a queryable history feed. Stories 15, 16, and 17 must land in that numeric order only insofar as each edits `app/(pages)/automation/page.tsx` a second/third/fourth time (a mechanical merge concern, not a functional dependency) — Story 14 is the only hard prerequisite for all three.

Stories 18–21 implement KAN-6 ("Knowledge Base"), mirroring the backend's own four-story split (core CRUD, publishing, guide steps, search) one-to-one — each backend story extends the same single `KnowledgeArticle` aggregate rather than introducing a separate table per content type. Story 18 adds a new top-level `/knowledge-base` page and sidebar entry, reusing `/automation`'s exact list-panel/modal-form/pagination convention (`SlaPoliciesPanel`/`SlaPolicyForm` as the literal template) for one panel instead of stacking several — every article (FAQ, help article, or guide) is one row in one list, distinguished by a `type` filter rather than by separate pages. Story 19 is the first story in this codebase to add a genuinely public, unauthenticated surface (`app/widget/knowledge-base/`, alongside the existing `app/widget/live-chat/` precedent) — publishing an article via a button on the Story 18 list makes it visible there; the agent-facing and customer-facing pages are both extended incrementally by Stories 20–21 in lockstep with the backend's own trailing-field DTO extensions (`steps` in Story 20, unaffected DTO shape in Story 21). Story 20 introduces the first `/knowledge-base/[id]` detail page — needed only because ordered step management (add/edit/delete on a `Guide`'s steps) doesn't fit in Story 18's list-row modal — and extends the public detail page (Story 19) to render the same steps read-only. Story 21 adds a search box to the public list page only (`/widget/knowledge-base`), not the agent-facing management list, since the backend's search endpoint is `Published`-only by design and the acceptance criterion it satisfies ("full-text search across all knowledge base content") is customer-facing; the agent management page keeps Story 18's exact-match `type`/`status`/`category` filters instead. Stories 18–21 must land in that numeric order — each depends on types/pages the previous one created — unlike KAN-5's Stories 15–17, which were only mechanically ordered.

Stories 22–26 implement KAN-7 ("AI Features"), one story per acceptance criterion, mirroring the backend's own five-story split (Stories 25–29 in `azm-crm-backend`) one-to-one — each backend story is already implemented on that repo's `feature/kan-7-ai-features` branch, so every story below is a pure frontend consumer of an existing endpoint, with no new frontend abstraction shared across all five the way KAN-6's `KnowledgeArticle` aggregate was shared across its four stories:

- **Story 22** ("Generate AI ticket summaries") adds `aiSummary`/`aiSummaryGeneratedOn` to the `Ticket` type and a `TicketAiSummaryPanel` on `/tickets/[id]` — a button-triggered `POST /api/tickets/{id}/ai-summary` call followed by `router.refresh()`, the same "mutate, then let the page's own fetch pick up the new value" shape `EscalateTicketControl` (KAN-2 Story 07) established. It is the first of three new panels stacked into a new "AI" section on the ticket detail page, and every other KAN-7 story except 24 and 26 depends on it only for that shared insertion point, not for any code it produces.
- **Story 23** ("Suggest replies based on ticket context") adds a `TicketSuggestedReplyPanel` directly below Story 22's — a read-only, non-persisted `GET /api/tickets/{id}/suggested-reply` fetched on demand, displayed in a copyable textarea rather than wired into any conversation's composer, since no FK links `Ticket` (KAN-2) to `Conversation` (KAN-3) in this codebase — the same limitation KAN-4 Story 12's quick-reply-template picker never had to work around, because templates aren't ticket-scoped.
- **Story 24** ("Auto-categorize incoming tickets") is independent of Stories 22-23, 25, and 26 — it only touches the ticket **creation** form, widening `TicketFormValues.category` with a frontend-only `"Auto"` sentinel that, when chosen, omits `category` from the create request entirely so the backend's AI categorizer resolves it. It does not touch `/tickets/[id]` at all.
- **Story 25** ("Suggest solutions from knowledge base") adds a third, auto-loaded (not button-triggered, since its endpoint runs no LLM call) `TicketSuggestedArticlesPanel` below Story 23's, reusing the already-existing `KnowledgeArticlePublicListItem` type verbatim (the backend's `KnowledgeArticlePublicListItemDto` has an identical shape) and linking to the agent-facing `/knowledge-base/[id]` page from KAN-6 Story 18.
- **Story 26** ("Deploy AI chatbot for customer self-service") is the only KAN-7 story that doesn't touch `/tickets/[id]` — it adds a new public, unauthenticated `/widget/chatbot` page modeled directly on KAN-3 Story 09's `/widget/live-chat`, but turn-based over plain HTTP request/response instead of SignalR, since the backend does not extend `ChatHub` to the new `Chatbot` channel.
- Story 23 depends on Story 22 only for the shared ticket-detail-page insertion point (numbered sequentially to avoid a three-way merge on that file), not for any value or type it produces; Story 25 depends on Story 18 (KAN-6) for `KnowledgeArticlePublicListItem`/`/knowledge-base/[id]` and on Story 23 for the same insertion-point reason; Story 26 depends on Stories 08–09 (KAN-3) for `Message`/`MessageBubbleList` and the widget-page precedent, not on any other KAN-7 story.
- **Out of scope across all five KAN-7 stories**: everything the backend itself already scoped out (see the `azm-crm-backend` plan overview's identical section) — summary/suggestion history or versioning, a confidence score or human-confirmation step for AI-resolved categories, semantic/embedding-based KB relevance ranking, real-time delivery of chatbot messages, and any UI marking which fields were AI-generated versus manually entered, since the backend persists no such marker for any of them.
